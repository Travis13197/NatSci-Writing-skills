# Worked case: the mirror-world hypothesis Perspective

Version 1.2.2; updated 2026-11-23. This is a project-derived blueprint, not a template to copy verbatim. It records how a large conceptual project became a Nature Perspective and which lessons transfer to future hypothesis manuscripts.

## 1. Project setup

- Source hierarchy: design.md as the primary conceptual source; design-ref1–4 as supporting sources; author's Chinese LCY versions as the authoritative wording.
- Genre: Nature Perspective, not a Research Article.
- Final package: title, author placeholders, abstract, Introduction, six numbered sections, Box 1, Data/Code availability, 59 references, Acknowledgements, Competing interests, AI assistance statement and three figure legends.
- Production: official publisher template, double-spaced 12-pt Times New Roman, native Word equations, tracked changes with yellow highlights, unchanged source files.

## 2. Thesis

The mirror-world hypothesis proposes that molecular states, operations and measurable responses acquire corresponding mathematical descriptions in physical biology and learned representations. Physicochemical constraints and interactions recorded through data shape an effective representation geometry. The theoretical task is to discover variables that connect molecular composition, conformational populations, interactions and temporal change, then test whether relations found in representation space predict independent molecular measurements and guide interventions.

The memorable framing: AI models become a mathematical microscope for discovering the laws of life, expanding the molecular distinctions available to theory.

## 3. Section architecture

| Section | Job | Key move |
|---|---|---|
| Introduction | establish the explanatory gap and the theoretical opportunity | sequence–structure–interaction–function relations need a common quantitative description |
| 1. Learning the laws of biomolecules | define states, representations and operations | molecular processes become organized changes in state, population, interaction or response |
| 2. Two worlds, one molecular language | state the mirror correspondence | physical constraints shape representation geometry; preserved relations are candidates for biological meaning |
| 3. How molecular processes shape representation geometry | build the method | operations probe state space; differential/information geometry and time-resolved analysis describe local relations |
| 4. Finding persistent relationships across models | test robustness | cross-model comparisons ask which relations survive coordinate changes |
| 5. How laws acquire biological meaning and return to molecules | close the loop | independent measurements give meaning; interventions test predictions |
| 6. Mathematical microscopes and new horizons | state the consequence | a representation-mediated theoretical biology and a broader AI4S programme |
| Box 1 | define the decoding scheme | three levels: variables, relations, biological meaning |
| Figures 1–3 | carry the argument | mirror world; operations to response geometry; states, paths and interventions |

## 4. Mathematical and evidence objects

The project separated established thermodynamics from proposed mappings. Core objects included:
- response laws and operational state equivalence;
- a collision bound for compressed representations;
- exact mean-pooling decomposition for sequence extension;
- sparse/supervised variable discovery;
- cross-model invariants (CKA, orthogonal Procrustes);
- Fisher information, rank constraints and pullback;
- Markov/VAMP closure and two-state rates;
- an adenylate-kinase-inspired four-state equilibrium example distinguishing a known thermodynamic relation from a proposed score-to-population mapping;
- KRAS/LplA and nucleic-acid/cell extensions as scope tests.

Lesson: a Perspective can carry serious mathematics when every object has a definition, an assumption, a biological interpretation and a failure condition. Long derivations belong in a methods note; the main text keeps the relation that changes the argument.

## 5. Figures and box

The three figures were designed as one argument:
1. **Mirror world:** left, physical molecules and operations; right, learned representation geometry; the two sides connected by a two-way correspondence.
2. **From operations to response geometry:** mutation squares, sequence extension, local directions, statistical metric, path/curvature and cross-model comparison.
3. **States, paths and interventions:** an empirical law, variable z, the AdK equilibrium model, a compensatory double variant, relaxation rates, response collisions, calibration and a discovery–interpretation–intervention–measurement loop.

Visual rules used: pure white background, flat vector-like, no gradients/3D, limited palette, at least 75% diagram area, fewer than about 45 words, panel letters a–h, explicit "conceptual; no measured results" footer. Prompts specified the composition, exact text inventory and scientific constraints; generated images still require manual checking of labels, equations and molecular shapes.

## 6. Production lessons

- The author's Chinese text is authoritative. Light polish changes grammar and collocation; expansion adds explanation without replacing the author's terms or claim order.
- Copy the source before a revision; use tracked changes and yellow highlights; never edit the only copy.
- Citations go before the period when that is the author's convention; keep the numbering identical in both languages.
- Bold two or three single-sentence insights per subsection; bold is for the argument, not decoration.
- Put boundaries in one paragraph. Caveats distributed through every paragraph make a Perspective read like a Research Article and weaken the thesis.
- Explain mathematical terms in biological language. Replace protocol detail with the conceptual relation; move the protocol to Methods.
- Figure prompts are design documents, not generated truth. Check every label, arrow, equation and disclaimer; do not show unperformed experiments as data.
- The AI assistance statement must describe the real scope: drafting, language revision, source comparison, mathematical checking, translation and assembly.

## 6A. Derivative products

The same thesis supports a book proposal or a broader AI4S programme statement. Keep the scientific core, widen the consequence carefully, and answer the editor's "why this, why now, why this press" question with a field-level argument. Personal conviction can be explicit; evidence claims stay honest, precedents stay verified, and the proposal does not present a future programme as completed results.

## 7. Reusable lessons

1. Start from a tension, not a topic.
2. Define the objects before using them.
3. Give the conceptual move one sentence the reader can remember.
4. Separate established, definitional, model-dependent, hypothetical and prospective claims.
5. Make the mathematics answer a biological question.
6. Design figures as an argument, not as a collage.
7. Keep one boundary paragraph.
8. Preserve the author's wording and claims when editing.
9. Test the bilingual pair for claim, number and certainty parity.
10. Audit the manuscript across computer science, mathematics and life science before submission.
