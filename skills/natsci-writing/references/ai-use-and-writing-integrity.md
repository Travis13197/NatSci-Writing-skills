# AI use, disclosure and writing integrity

Version 1.2.5; updated 2026-11-24. Read whenever AI/LLM tools are used to draft, revise, translate, search, code or prepare figures for a manuscript. Journal policies override every default here; verify the current target-journal policy before submission.

## 1. The non-negotiables

1. **AI cannot be an author.** Nature Portfolio, Science/AAAS, ICMJE, COPE, WAME and major publishers all state that AI tools cannot take responsibility, consent or accountability, so they cannot be listed as authors or co-authors.
2. **Human authors remain fully responsible** for originality, accuracy, integrity, citations, data, images, permissions, ethics and the final text, whether or not AI was used.
3. **Use must be disclosed** in the form and place the target journal requires. Undisclosed substantive AI use is a integrity risk even when the prose is good.
4. **AI must not fabricate or substitute for evidence.** No invented references, datasets, results, mechanisms, statistics, reviewer identities, approval numbers or line numbers.
5. **Confidential material stays out of public AI tools.** Do not upload unpublished manuscripts, grant applications, peer-review material, personal or patient data, or restricted third-party data to services that may retain or train on it.
6. **AI-generated images must not be presented as data.** Do not generate or alter microscopy, gels, blots, imaging, structures or quantitative plots and present them as experimental results. Illustrative AI images require disclosure and must not imply measurement.

## 2. What is normally acceptable

With human verification and journal-compliant disclosure:

- language editing: grammar, clarity, concision, tone, translation with terminology checks;
- structural help: outlines, paragraph maps, reverse outlines, alternative titles or abstract phrasings that the authors then judge and rewrite;
- literature triage: building search strings, de-duplicating records, classifying abstracts for manual screening (not as a substitute for reading);
- formatting: reference-style conversion, table/figure checklist generation, journal-format compliance checks;
- code assistance: drafting or debugging analysis or figure code, with outputs reproduced and validated by the authors;
- figure layout suggestions: panel arrangement, colour/typography checks, caption structure; the authors make the scientific decisions and verify every number.

## 3. What is normally not acceptable

- Listing AI as an author, contributor or corresponding contact.
- Using AI to generate substantive scientific claims, mechanisms, results or interpretations without evidence and human verification.
- Fabricating or "completing" references, DOIs, accession numbers, statistics or approvals.
- Generating or manipulating research images or data.
- Uploading unpublished or confidential manuscripts or review material to a public model.
- Using AI to write peer-review reports in place of independent judgement; reviewers generally must not upload manuscripts to public LLMs.
- Using AI to evade detection, disguise authorship or hide the extent of assistance.
- Presenting AI-generated text as the authors' original wording without disclosure when the journal requires it.

## 4. Journal positions to check (current as of November 2026)

| Body | Core position | Typical disclosure/placement |
|---|---|---|
| Nature Portfolio | AI cannot be author; authors responsible; AI use in writing should be documented | Methods or Acknowledgements; AI-generated images restricted; confidentiality of manuscripts |
| Science/AAAS | AI cannot be author; authors responsible; AI-generated research images prohibited; reviewers must not upload manuscripts to LLMs; disclosed language polishing may be allowed | Journal-specific editorial policy; check current instructions |
| ICMJE | AI cannot be author; authors responsible; AI-assisted writing should be described | Cover letter/acknowledgements or Methods as the journal requires; describe use and oversight |
| COPE | AI cannot be author; authors accountable; transparency required | Journal-specific disclosure |
| Elsevier | AI cannot be author; disclose generative AI use in manuscript preparation | Separate AI declaration at submission; basic spelling/grammar checks may not need declaration, but check the journal |
| Springer Nature | AI cannot be author; responsible use and disclosure required | Methods/Acknowledgements or journal-specific declaration |

Policy details and placements change. Record the exact policy URL and date checked in the submission notes; do not import one publisher's rule into another journal.

## 5. Disclosure that is specific enough

A useful disclosure answers: which tool, which version/date, what it was used for, which sections or tasks, how outputs were checked, and who takes responsibility.

Template:

"We used [tool, version] for [language editing / literature triage / code drafting / figure layout] on [sections]. The authors verified all outputs against the underlying data and sources, revised the text, and take full responsibility for the content. No AI tool was used to generate data, images or references."

Rules:
- Describe the function, not a vague "AI-assisted".
- State the sections or tasks, not only the tool name.
- State the verification performed.
- Do not include AI as an author or contributor.
- Basic spelling/grammar checks may not require disclosure under some policies; follow the target journal rather than guessing.
- Keep the disclosure consistent with Methods, Acknowledgements, cover letter and submission system.

## 6. AI-assisted writing without an AI voice

AI assistance is allowed only if the final text still reads as a scientist's argument:

- Every claim maps to evidence the authors can defend.
- Every citation is verified against the actual source; AI-suggested references are treated as unverified leads.
- Numbers are recomputed or checked against source data, never accepted from the model.
- Paragraphs make one argument each; no generic padding, triads, "not only ... but also", "in the realm of", or promissory endings.
- The authors' terminology, judgement and voice dominate; the model does not introduce new concepts or scope.
- Confidence matches evidence; the text does not inflate novelty or hide limitations.
- The final text is read line by line by a human who can explain each sentence.

## 7. Workflow gates

1. **Before use**: check the target journal's AI policy; decide what may be outsourced; remove confidential material.
2. **During use**: keep a task log (tool, version, date, purpose, sections); never paste restricted data; ask for alternatives rather than accepting the first output.
3. **Evidence gate**: verify every citation, number and factual claim; keep a claim-evidence ledger.
4. **Language gate**: remove AI-tone markers, generic transitions and unsupported adjectives; restore the authors' voice.
5. **Disclosure gate**: draft the journal-specific statement; place it where required; keep it consistent across files.
6. **Final gate**: a human reads the complete manuscript and can defend every sentence; image/data integrity is checked separately.

## 8. Red flags in an AI-assisted manuscript

- References that look plausible but do not resolve, or citations that do not support the sentence.
- Numbers or statistics that change between abstract, Results, figures and supplement.
- Paragraphs that summarise the field without a specific question, or repeat the same claim in different words.
- Generic transitions and evaluative adjectives instead of comparisons and measurements.
- Methods that describe standard practice without the actual parameters, software or versions.
- Figures or images that look synthetic, inconsistent in lighting/scale, or have impossible labels.
- Disclosure that names a tool but not the task, or claims "no AI was used" when it was.

## 9. AI-use audit

- Target-journal AI policy checked (URL, date).
- AI cannot be author/contributor; authorship list human-only.
- Every AI-assisted task logged and disclosed as required.
- No confidential/unpublished material uploaded to a public tool.
- No fabricated references, data, images, statistics or approvals.
- Every citation and number verified; claim-evidence ledger complete.
- Final text read and defended by a human author.
- Disclosure consistent across manuscript, cover letter and submission system.
- AI-generated images, if any, are illustrative, disclosed and not presented as data.

## 10. AI writing negative list (zero tolerance)

- AI listed as author, co-author, contributor or corresponding contact.
- Fabricated references, DOIs, ISBNs, URLs, accession numbers, trial registrations or ethics approvals.
- Citations that do not support the sentence, or citation padding to simulate scholarship.
- Hallucinated methods, parameters, software versions, sample sizes, statistics or results.
- Fabricated data, images, microscopy, gels, blots, structures or source data.
- Text recycling, self-plagiarism, duplicate submission or salami slicing.
- Paper-mill patterns: tortured phrases, template abstracts, fake affiliations, purchased authorship, citation manipulation.
- AI-generated peer-review reports, editorial decisions or reviewer identities.
- Uploading unpublished, confidential, peer-review, patient or third-party material to a public model.
- Prompt injection or hidden instructions in a submitted file.
- AI paraphrasing to evade detection, disguise authorship or hide the extent of assistance.
- Undisclosed translation, ghostwriting or substantial AI drafting.
- Fake funding, conflicts of interest, acknowledgements, author contributions or permissions.
- AI-generated cover letters, highlights or significance statements that overstate the paper.
- Using AI to write the entire manuscript without substantive human analysis, interpretation and verification.
- Claiming "no AI was used" when it was, or naming a tool without describing the task and oversight.
- Copyright or licence violations: copyrighted text/images, restricted data, tool terms that forbid publication.
- Unverified AI-suggested collaborators, reviewers or editors.
- AI-generated images presented as data or as completed experiments (see ai-figures-and-visual-integrity.md).

## 11. 2026 policy clarifications to verify

- **Nature Portfolio:** Nature Methods (2026) states that generative AI may help write or edit sections, with declaration in Methods; Nature Materials (2026) asks authors to declare other AI uses in Methods with the model and training described. AI-generated images must not misrepresent data; some Nature Portfolio journals prohibit AI-generated images and videos.
- **Science/AAAS:** AI-generated text, images and graphics require explicit editor permission; undisclosed AI use in visual content is inconsistent with editorial standards. The 2026 cover-image correction shows that the policy applies to cover imagery as well as figures.
- **Elsevier:** the June 2026 update requires AI use in each image to be disclosed in that image's caption (tool, version, how used) and in the general AI declaration. Basic grammar/spelling checks may not need a declaration; substantive restructuring does.
- **Cell Press:** AI-generated figures must be clearly labelled; AI in data figures requires editorial review.
- **PLOS:** AI content is allowed with disclosure when it does not misrepresent primary research, introduce invalid content or expose personal/sensitive data.
- **ICMJE (2026):** authors should disclose if, where and how AI was used, including language and grammar correction; journals should state their AI policy; AI cannot be an author.
- **COPE:** AI tools cannot be authors; authors remain accountable; undeclared AI image creation is an integrity case, not a formatting issue.
- **Springer Nature:** aligns with Nature Portfolio; reproducibility and human oversight are central.

## 12. Figures and images

AI-generated or AI-assisted figures have their own policy, provenance and negative checklist. Read ai-figures-and-visual-integrity.md before using any generative image tool. Key defaults: data figures come from real data and code; conceptual schematics may use AI with disclosure and human verification; no generative fill or synthetic research images; archive prompt, tool/version, output hash and manual edits.

## 13. Reviewer and editor boundaries

Reviewers should not upload manuscripts, figures or unpublished data to public AI tools; AI-generated review reports are generally unacceptable; the reviewer remains responsible for the judgement. Editors and publishers apply their own AI policies to editorial content. Authors should not treat editorial AI use as permission for their own use.

## 14. Detection, translation and licensing

AI-text detectors are unreliable; do not optimize for evading them. Optimize for accuracy, specificity and human responsibility. Disclose translation assistance when the journal requires it; the translated text must preserve claims, numbers, citations, certainty and emphasis. Check the tool's terms for input/output rights, training use, commercial restrictions and publication permissions. Confirm that the publisher's licence permits all generated or third-party content.

## 15. Additional audit checks

- AI writing negative list checked item by item.
- AI figure provenance record exists for every AI-assisted image.
- Journal policy URL/date recorded; disclosure placement verified.
- All AI-suggested references, numbers and methods verified.
- Confidential and patient material kept out of public tools.
- Reviewer/editor boundaries respected.
- Copyright, licence and tool terms checked.
- AI detection not used as a substitute for human review.
