# Nature Perspective and theory-manuscript production

Version 1.2.2; updated 2026-11-23. Read for a Nature/Science/Cell-style Perspective, hypothesis paper, conceptual Review or theory manuscript. Pair with perspective-and-insight.md (intellectual method), editorial-and-submission.md (journal fit) and writing-diagnostics.md section 3A (zero-tolerance style).

## 1. What this genre is

A Perspective argues for a change in how a field understands a problem. It presents a thesis, the conceptual move that supports it, the evidence that makes it credible, the experiments that could test it and the consequences if it holds. A research article reports completed experiments; a Perspective proposes a way to think and a way to test.

The manuscript still needs a rigorous evidence architecture. Separate what is established, what follows from a definition or model, what is proposed as an empirical hypothesis, and what remains a future experiment. The prose can be ambitious; the claim status must stay exact.

## 2. Editorial contract to verify

For Nature Perspective, the current guide follows Review format: preface/abstract up to about 200 words, main text generally under 7,000 words, about four display items, references usually no more than 150, and a short bold annotation for 5–10% of key references. Editors set the final length. Verify the current guide at submission; do not import Research Article limits.

The project-derived manuscript used: 3 conceptual figures + 1 box, 59 references, 21 single-sentence bold key statements across seven subsections, and a 184-word English abstract at one stage. Treat these as an example of a conforming package, not a universal quota.

## 2A. Read exemplars before designing

Before drafting, read three to five target-journal Perspectives in full: abstract, introduction, all substantive sections, boxes, figure legends and ending. Map the opening puzzle, the conceptual move, the section sequence, where evidence is placed, how speculation is marked and how the piece ends. Separate recurring practice from one article's structure. A technical roadmap and a conceptual essay can both be Perspectives; their shared contribution is a sustained viewpoint with consequences for inquiry.

## 3. Thesis and architecture

Write one editorial sentence before drafting:

> After this article, a reader will understand that [relationship/mechanism/framing] is [different] because [conceptual move], with consequences for [measurement/experiment/design].

A six-section archetype for a theory Perspective:

1. **Tension.** A concrete explanatory gap, contradiction or failure of current framing.
2. **Definitions.** The objects, operations and observables; what can be measured.
3. **Conceptual move.** The proposed correspondence, mechanism or reframing, stated plainly.
4. **Method.** The mathematical or empirical machinery that makes the move testable.
5. **Return.** How the proposed relation acquires biological meaning and changes the next experiment.
6. **Horizon.** What a field would gain, with boundaries concentrated in one paragraph.

Each section delivers a distinct intellectual result. The sections are not a literature review, a method catalogue or a protocol. Every section must change what the reader can ask or do.

## 4. Full-paragraph rule

Manuscript prose uses connected paragraphs. Do not put bullet lists, numbered steps or sub-bullets inside a manuscript paragraph or a section of argument. Use a list only for genuinely enumerable items outside the argument (author checklist, reagent list, supplementary inventory), and keep it out of the submitted narrative.

Each paragraph:
- opens with the claim, question or move it contributes;
- develops one line of reasoning with concrete objects, comparisons and evidence;
- ends with the inference, the next question or a consequence;
- stays readable for a biologist outside the subfield.

Key sentences: bold two or three single sentences per subsection when the target journal permits. Use them for the core concept, the key inference or the memorable insight. Do not bold whole paragraphs or use bold as decoration.

## 4A. House rules from the author's system prompt

These rules apply to the whole deliverable:
- Write as a top expert in artificial intelligence and biotechnology, not as a generic assistant.
- Meet the highest Nature/Science academic standard in argument, evidence, language and figures.
- Use complete paragraphs. Do not put sub-bullets, numbered steps or list fragments inside a manuscript paragraph.
- Remove AI flavor, especially self-limiting contrast constructions: "is/does/do not X, but Y", "not X, but Y", "not X, rather than Y", and the Chinese equivalents "不是……而是……", "并不……而是……", "不……而是……".
- Deliver the complete manuscript or complete figure set, not a sketch or an outline, unless the user asks for a plan.

## 5. Evidence hierarchy and claim calibration

Keep five levels explicit in your own ledger:

| Level | What it is | How to write it |
|---|---|---|
| Established | published measurement or accepted theory | declarative, with source |
| Definitional | follows from a stated definition | "we define", "by construction" |
| Model-dependent | follows from stated assumptions | name the assumptions and the consequence |
| Empirical hypothesis | proposed relation to be tested | "we propose", "the hypothesis predicts" |
| Prospective experiment | future test | "would test", "a prospective experiment" |

Never let a model-derived relation become an observed result. Never describe a proposed experiment as completed. Cite the closest prior work and state what the new paper adds: a relation, an operationalization, a test or an integration.

Boundaries: put them in one dedicated paragraph or section. State the condition, the scope and the consequence once. Do not attach a caveat to every paragraph; caveat creep weakens the thesis and reads as defensive.

## 6. Mathematics in a conceptual paper

Mathematics earns its place by unlocking a deduction, not by displaying sophistication. For each formal object:
- define the symbol, its domain and its units;
- state the assumptions needed for the displayed result;
- distinguish algebraic identity, model consequence and empirical hypothesis;
- connect the object to a measurable biological quantity;
- state what would make the relation fail.

Useful objects in the mirror-world project illustrate the standard: response laws, operational state equivalence, collision bounds, mean-pooling decomposition, cross-model invariants, Fisher information and rank constraints, Markov closure, and an AdK-inspired equilibrium example. Each was written with its assumptions and its experimental meaning. Keep long derivations in a Methods/Supplementary note and retain only the relation that changes the argument in the main text.

## 7. Figures and box

Conceptual figures carry the argument. A three-figure archetype:

1. **The conceptual world.** The two-way correspondence, drawn as one integrated composition rather than a collage of unrelated panels.
2. **The method.** How operations become measurable relations, how geometry/invariants are computed, and where compression can fail.
3. **The return.** How a relation acquires biological meaning, selects an intervention and is tested.

Visual rules for a Nature-level conceptual figure:
- white background, flat vector-like artwork, no gradients, shadows, glow or 3D perspective;
- limited colour-blind-aware palette; consistent outlines and type;
- at least 75% of the canvas for diagrams; fewer than about 45 words of text;
- lowercase panel letters at aligned corners; one figure-level title in the caption, not in the image;
- a footer such as "Conceptual; no measured results" when the figure is illustrative;
- no fabricated molecular structures, error bars, significance marks or success rates.

Box: use one box for a compact formal definition, three-level decoding scheme or a self-contained derivation. A box is an argumentative instrument, not a glossary.

Figure legends: title sentence, panel-by-panel reading, symbol definitions, explicit conceptual/illustrative status. Put all legends at the end of the manuscript.

## 8. Bilingual workflow

When both languages are required:
1. Fix the Chinese argument first when the author's Chinese is the source of truth; otherwise fix the English argument first.
2. Build a term ledger: concept, Chinese term, English term, definition, first-use location.
3. Translate by paragraph, not by sentence. Preserve claim order, evidence status, numbers, citations, hedges and emphasis.
4. Write natural Chinese prose. Unfold English noun chains into subject-verb-object sentences; do not transliterate.
5. Read the two versions side by side and check: no added claim, no deleted qualification, no changed number, no citation moved across a claim.
6. Keep citations before the period if that is the author's or journal's convention; keep numbering identical across languages.
7. Quantitative parity: define each variable once, then report the same n, means, paired change, interval, direction and association-versus-causation status in both languages. A translation may not soften or strengthen the evidence.

## 9. Editing modes and fidelity

Before editing, agree on the mode:

| Mode | Allowed | Not allowed |
|---|---|---|
| Light polish | grammar, collocation, punctuation, minimal wording; preserve claim order and scope | new claims, new experiments, new methods, wholesale rewrite |
| Author-faithful expansion | preserve author wording and claims; add explanations where marked | changing the author's terms, order or thesis |
| Structural rewrite | only with explicit permission; preserve the claim set and evidence | introducing new science or deleting inconvenient evidence |
| Translation | semantic parity, term consistency | adding or removing claims, changing certainty |
| Tracked revision | copy the file; tracked changes/yellow highlights; source untouched | editing the source in place, silent rewrites |
| Audit | report issues and locations | rewriting the text without permission |

Light polish still returns a change log and lists residual questions. A noun-stack rewrite must preserve theoretical status: "We propose comparing whether selected relations retain corresponding expressions across models and relating them to time-resolved measurements" is a proposal; "framework operationalizes a validation pipeline" claims a completed system.

## 10. Production and format

- Use the target journal's current template; if unavailable, use the publisher's official author-services template and record its source, version and hash.
- Double-spaced 12-pt Times New Roman body unless the template specifies otherwise; line numbers and page numbers for review.
- Title, abstract, main text, box, references, declarations, figure legends in the required order.
- References numbered by first appearance; in-text citations before the period when that is the author's convention; verify every DOI and metadata record.
- Key references: annotate 5–10% with one bold sentence that says what the source establishes.
- Equations as native editable objects (OMML in Word), numbered only when displayed; check sub/superscripts, hats, fractions and conditionals.
- Author placeholders: never invent affiliation, correspondence, ORCID, funding, contributions or approvals. Mark them visibly.
- AI assistance: disclose the actual scope (drafting, language, literature comparison, math checking, translation, assembly), not "language polishing only".
- Read the final PDF page by page; check equations, figure placement, legends, references and placeholders.

## 11. Three-discipline review

Before submission, run three reviews:
- **Computer science / AI:** model identity, checkpoint, inputs, tokenization, layer/readout, training data, leakage, baselines.
- **Mathematics:** definitions, assumptions, identifiability, invariant vs coordinate-dependent statement, error bounds, limiting cases.
- **Life science:** biological meaning of each variable, measurement, alternative explanations, feasibility, scope.

Then a claim-evidence ledger: every central claim → evidence → status → boundary → location. Flag any claim with no evidence, any proposed experiment written as a result, and any citation that does not support its sentence.

## 12. Anti-patterns from the project

| Pattern | Why it fails | Rewrite |
|---|---|---|
| noun stacking | reader cannot tell what acts on what | use subject-verb-object; define each term |
| caveat creep | every paragraph hedges; thesis disappears | one boundaries paragraph; local conditions where needed |
| over-precision | protocol detail replaces the idea | state the conceptual relation; move protocol to Methods |
| self-limiting framing | "provides clues", "may help", "preliminary" undersell a Perspective | state the proposed consequence directly |
| method catalogue | lists models/tools without an argument | each method answers a question |
| false novelty | "first", "universal", "law" without search | name the closest work and the incremental contribution |
| figure collage | unrelated panels without a figure-level claim | one integrated composition with panel roles |
| rewriting the author | changes wording, claims and order | preserve author text; mark additions; use the agreed mode |

## 13. Checklist

- One-sentence thesis and one conceptual move.
- Six-section architecture with a distinct result per section.
- Full paragraphs; no lists inside the argument.
- Key sentences bold, two or three per subsection, single sentences.
- Evidence hierarchy explicit; no proposed experiment reported as a result.
- Boundaries in one paragraph, not distributed as defensive caveats.
- Every equation has symbols, assumptions, units and biological meaning.
- Three figures form one argument; white/flat/limited palette; conceptual disclaimer; legends at end.
- Chinese and English have identical claims, numbers, citations and status.
- Target-journal format verified; references before periods; key reference annotations; placeholders marked.
- AI disclosure matches the actual use.
- Three-discipline review and claim-evidence ledger complete.
- tools/check_prose_style.py reports no ERROR hits.
