# Editorial fit, submission package and journal requirements

Version 1.1.0; updated 2026-11-23. Read this file when choosing a target journal, judging editorial fit, preparing an initial submission, writing a cover letter, or auditing whether a manuscript meets Nature/Science-level editorial criteria. Journal instructions override every default here; record the stage and verify current guidance before submission.

## 1. Editorial criteria (what editors are asked to judge)

Flagship Nature evaluates original research Articles against four broad criteria:

1. **Originality** — original scientific research whose main results and conclusions are not published or simultaneously submitted elsewhere.
2. **Scientific importance** — outstanding importance, not competent incremental progress.
3. **Interdisciplinary interest** — a conclusion that matters to readers beyond the immediate specialty.
4. **Technical soundness and readability** — a case that is technically established and intelligible to non-specialists.

Additional fit tests used in practice:
- A large-dataset paper needs comprehensive validation, a significant technical advance, or a scientific insight; size alone is not importance.
- A purely technical paper needs demonstrable impact for a research community.
- A therapeutic paper without a new mechanism needs a substantial effect on an important disease.

Science applies the same family of judgements in its own editorial language: a specific advance, significance to a broad readership, technical rigour and clear presentation. Verify the current Science guide rather than importing Nature's wording.

**Rule:** editorial fit is a scientific argument, not a formatting claim. If the fit is weak, say so and propose how to strengthen the contribution or choose a better venue; do not inflate adjectives.

## 2. Stage gate

Record the active stage before applying any rule:

| Stage | What is required | What is not required |
|---|---|---|
| Initial submission | complete, reviewable manuscript; figures legible; declarations and availability statements present | final production typography, editable production artwork, final ED formatting |
| Revision | point-by-point response; revised manuscript; editor instructions plus public guide | proof-level polish |
| Accepted in principle | production text, production-quality figures and ED, final SI, declarations, forms | new scientific claims |
| Proof | correction of production errors | rewriting the science |

Do not fail an initial submission for missing final-production styling. Do fail it for missing scientific content, unreadable or misrepresented data, absent required statements, undisclosed overlap or inaccessible claim-critical data/code.

## 3. Article limits (verify at submission)

| Item | Nature Article | Nature Machine Intelligence Article | Science Research Article |
|---|---|---|---|
| Title | ≤75 characters including spaces, normally ≤2 printed lines | check current guide | check current guide |
| Abstract / summary | fully referenced summary ≤~200 words; avoid numbers and abbreviations unless essential | unreferenced abstract ≤150 words | ~125 words, 5–6 sentences |
| Main text | ~2,500 words (6 pages, 4 display items) or ~4,300 words (8 pages, 5–6 display items) | ≤3,500 words (Introduction+Results+Discussion) | no fixed page model; results-led |
| References | ≤~50 main-text references | typically ≤50 | journal style |
| Subheadings | ≤40 characters including spaces | short topical subheadings in Results/Methods; none in Discussion | check guide |
| Methods | concise; typically ≤3,000 words; separate Data/Code Availability | concise, reproducible; online Methods | journal style |
| Figure legends | <250 words at initial submission; production rules differ | check NMI guide | check guide |
| Extended Data / SI | ≤10 ED multi-panel items; SI for essential large/specialised material | ED ≤10; SI when needed | journal style |
| Structure | title, authors, affiliations, summary, main text, references, tables, legends, Methods, statements, ED/SI | unheaded Introduction, Results, Discussion, Methods | abstract, text, references, supplementary |

Do not import flagship Nature numbers into Nature subjournals or vice versa. NMI, Nature Communications and field journals have their own limits.

## 5. Cover letter

Anatomy (250–400 words unless the journal specifies otherwise):

1. Submission sentence: title, article type, journal.
2. What the study addresses and the principal finding (1–2 sentences).
3. What is specifically new and which evidence supports it (1–2 sentences).
4. Why it matters to this journal's readership (1 sentence).
5. Required declarations: originality, author approval, related manuscripts/preprints, conflicts.
6. Corresponding author and contact.

Rules:
- Do not repeat the abstract.
- Do not use unsupported priority claims ("first", "unprecedented").
- Do not name-drop editors or famous researchers.
- Connect the finding to the journal's scope and readers; do not assert fit without a reason.
- For Nature, the initial cover letter is optional and its absence is not a blocker; for NMI it is part of the package.
- Never reuse a revision cover letter template for initial submission.

## 6. Presubmission enquiry and journal choice

A presubmission enquiry may ask whether a full submission is likely to be competitive. It needs a broad-readership cover paragraph, a fully referenced summary paragraph and a reference list. NMI does not consider presubmission enquiries; prepare the full package.

Journal choice:
- Match scope and readership, not prestige alone.
- Compare the paper's strongest claim with the journal's editorial criteria and recent articles.
- Consider Nature-family transfer options only after an editorial decision, and follow the editor's instructions.
- If the advance is field-specific but rigorous, a specialised journal may serve the work better than a flagship attempt.

## 7. Administrative and integrity checks

- All authors agree with authorship, order and submission; corresponding author is identified.
- Related manuscripts with overlapping authorship are disclosed and supplied.
- Preprints are disclosed with DOI and licence; the accepted version may be posted only under the journal's policy.
- Double-anonymous review, when selected, requires removing author identities, self-citations that reveal identity, acknowledgements, repository links and file metadata.
- Reviewer suggestions are independent, with verified affiliations, emails and no recent collaboration or institutional conflict.
- LLM/AI use is disclosed according to the journal's policy; AI cannot be an author.
- Reporting summary, data/code statements and ethics approvals are complete and consistent with the manuscript.

## 8. Submission readiness

Return one of:
- ready: all required facts and files are present and cross-checked;
- ready_with_author_checks: drafts complete, administrative facts need confirmation;
- blocked: required ethics, authorship, permissions, integrity or journal-rule information is missing.

## 9. Final editorial audit

- Does the manuscript meet the journal's originality, importance, breadth and soundness criteria in one sentence each?
- Is the advance stated without unsupported priority or universality claims?
- Does the summary/abstract answer why this, why now and why broad?
- Are title, abstract, cover letter and significance statement consistent?
- Are all limits, structure, references, legends and statements compliant?
- Are the data/code/materials access routes real and sufficient for review?
- Are related work, preprints and reviewer suggestions disclosed correctly?
- Is the package complete at the recorded stage, with missing facts listed rather than invented?
