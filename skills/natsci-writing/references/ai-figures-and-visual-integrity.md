# AI-generated and AI-assisted figures: policy, provenance and integrity

Version 1.2.5; updated 2026-11-24. Read before using any generative image model, AI layout tool or AI-assisted image processing for a manuscript, graphical abstract, cover or slide. Journal policy overrides every default here; verify the target journal before generation.

## 1. First classify the figure

The policy depends on what the figure claims.

| Class | Examples | Default |
|---|---|---|
| Data figure | microscopy, gels, blots, imaging, spectra, plots, structures, maps | Generate from real data with code; never with a generative image model |
| Conceptual schematic | mechanism diagram, workflow, hypothesis figure, theory schematic | May use AI with disclosure, human verification and a conceptual label |
| Graphical abstract / cover | journal graphical abstract, cover art, social image | Editor permission and journal-specific AI policy; disclose |
| AI-assisted layout | arranging panels, typography, colour, export | Allowed; disclose when substantive; verify final vector and data |
| AI-assisted processing | denoising, segmentation, registration, contrast adjustment | Research method; describe in Methods, keep raw data, avoid generative fill |

An AI-generated image is not a data figure because it looks quantitative. A generative model that invents a blot, a microscopy field, a molecule or a plot has fabricated data.

## 2. Publisher positions to check

| Publisher | Current position (verify at submission) |
|---|---|
| Nature Portfolio | AI cannot be author; disclose AI use in Methods; AI-generated images must not misrepresent data; some Nature Portfolio journals prohibit AI-generated images/videos; non-generative ML image processing may be allowed with caption disclosure; reviewers may request verification |
| Science/AAAS | AI-generated text, images and graphics require explicit editor permission; undisclosed AI use in visual content is inconsistent with editorial standards; authors remain responsible; cover imagery follows the same policy |
| Cell Press | AI-generated figures must be clearly labelled; AI in data figures requires editorial review |
| Elsevier | AI cannot be author; declare tool, purpose and oversight at submission; as of June 2026, AI use in an image must also be disclosed in that image's caption (tool, version, how used) and in the general AI statement |
| Springer Nature | Aligns with Nature Portfolio; reproducibility and human oversight emphasized |
| PLOS | AI content allowed with disclosure when it does not misrepresent primary research and does not expose personal or sensitive data |
| ICMJE / COPE | AI cannot be author; authors are accountable; disclose use; image integrity follows the same responsibility |

Policies are moving. Record the exact URL and date checked. Do not assume one journal's exception applies to another.

## 3. Absolute prohibitions

- Generating, altering or "completing" research images with a generative model: microscopy, gels, blots, histology, imaging, spectroscopy, structures, maps, quantitative plots or source data.
- Generative fill, outpainting, object removal, face replacement or scene synthesis in a research image.
- Selective enhancement, splicing, duplication, erasing or adding elements that changes interpretation; global linear adjustment with disclosure is a different matter.
- AI upscaling that invents detail and then presents it as acquired resolution.
- Presenting a synthetic image as measured data, or drawing error bars, significance marks, scale bars or success rates that were not measured.
- Fabricated molecular structures, cells, organs, devices or experimental setups presented as real.
- Undisclosed AI in cover art, graphical abstracts or figure panels.
- Uploading unpublished, confidential, patient, personal or third-party restricted images into a public model.
- Using copyrighted images, characters, logos or identifiable people as prompts or outputs without permission.
- Text labels generated by an image model and used without checking spelling, numbers and symbols.
- A figure with no provenance for a stochastic AI output.

## 4. What may be allowed, with conditions

- Conceptual schematics and theory diagrams: AI-assisted composition is acceptable when the science is specified by the authors, the image is labelled conceptual, every label and relationship is verified, and the journal permits it.
- Graphical abstracts: check the journal's AI and graphical-abstract policy; disclose; keep the scientific claim consistent with the paper; no fabricated data.
- Cover art: editor permission; disclosure; no implication of data; check copyright and licence.
- AI-assisted layout and typography: allowed; verify the final vector, fonts, panel letters, alignment and accessibility; disclose substantive assistance.
- AI code that draws a plot from verified data: this is a data figure produced by code, not an AI-generated image. Keep the code, data and environment; check the output against source data.
- Non-generative image processing (denoising, segmentation, registration): describe the method, parameters and software in Methods; keep raw files; disclose if the journal requires it.

## 5. Disclosure framework

Use three places when the journal permits AI-assisted figures:

1. **Methods subsection: AI-assisted figure generation.** Tool name, version, URL/backend, which figures, generation or editing process, date, human review, where prompts/metadata are archived.
2. **Figure legend.** A short note: "Created with [tool, version] for layout/conceptual illustration; labels and scientific content verified by the authors; generation record in Supplementary Data." Add "Conceptual; no measured results" when applicable.
3. **Cover letter.** State that AI was used for figure generation or layout and that the manuscript contains full disclosure.

Elsevier-style image caption: "AI tool [name, version] was used to [layout / conceptual drafting / colour adjustment] for this image; the authors verified all labels and content." Check the current policy for the exact required wording.

For a strict journal, a conceptual figure generated without permission may be rejected even with disclosure. Ask the editor before investing in cover art or graphical abstracts.

## 6. Provenance record

For every AI-assisted figure, keep:
- figure ID, manuscript section, figure class;
- tool name, version, backend/model, URL, generation date;
- full prompt and negative prompt, seed/settings if available;
- original output file at maximum resolution and SHA-256;
- manual edits: what was changed, in which editor, by whom;
- source data and code for any data-derived element;
- human reviewer and verification date;
- journal policy URL/date and disclosure placement;
- licence/terms of the tool and any third-party rights;
- archived prompt/metadata location (Supplementary Data, Zenodo, Figshare).

Stochastic models do not reproduce exactly. The record is the evidence of what was generated, reviewed and changed.

## 7. Human quality control

- Scientific accuracy: labels, arrows, spatial relationships, mechanisms and quantities.
- Text: spelling, numbers, symbols, units, gene/protein names, panel letters.
- Data integrity: every data-derived mark matches source data; no invented points, error bars or significance.
- Visual consistency: palette, line weights, type, scale and style match the other figures.
- Accessibility: colour-blind-safe palette, contrast, font size at final print size, no colour-only encoding.
- Composition: no overlaps, no clipped labels, no impossible perspective, no decorative clutter.
- Resolution and format: vector PDF/SVG where possible; 300–600 dpi raster; editable final file.
- Claim check: the figure supports the sentence it illustrates and does not imply a completed experiment.

## 8. Prompt and data hygiene

- Use the minimum data needed; remove names, faces, patient identifiers, unpublished sequences, grant text and third-party files.
- Prefer enterprise/zero-retention tools with terms that allow publication and do not train on your inputs.
- Never ask a model to invent a blot, a cell, a molecule, a patient image or a numerical result.
- Specify "conceptual schematic; no measured data" in the prompt, and render final labels in a vector editor.
- Avoid copyrighted characters, logos, photographs, album art or recognizable people.
- Keep prompts and outputs out of public repositories when they contain sensitive material.

## 9. Copyright and licensing

- Check the tool's terms for output ownership, commercial use, indemnity and restrictions on publication.
- Confirm that the publisher's licence permits the generated content and any third-party elements.
- Human authorship matters for copyright; AI-generated content may have limited protection in some jurisdictions.
- Keep licences and permission records with the provenance file.
- For cover art or graphical abstracts, confirm the journal's rights and attribution requirements.

## 10. Reviewer and editor boundaries

- Reviewers should not upload manuscripts, figures or unpublished data to public AI tools.
- AI-generated peer-review reports are generally not acceptable; the reviewer remains responsible for the judgement.
- Editors and publishers apply their own AI policies to editorial content; authors should not assume editorial AI use is permission for their own.
- If a reviewer requests AI-related clarification, answer with the provenance record and the disclosure.

## 11. Negative checklist

- [ ] AI-generated or AI-altered data image (microscopy, gel, blot, imaging, plot, structure).
- [ ] Generative fill, object removal or scene synthesis in a research image.
- [ ] Selective enhancement, duplication or splicing that changes interpretation.
- [ ] AI upscaling that invents detail.
- [ ] Synthetic image presented as measured data.
- [ ] Fake scale bar, error bar, significance mark or success rate.
- [ ] Fabricated molecule, cell, organ or device.
- [ ] Undisclosed AI in a figure, graphical abstract or cover.
- [ ] Confidential, patient or third-party image sent to a public model.
- [ ] Copyrighted character, logo, photo or identifiable person in a prompt/output.
- [ ] Unchecked AI-rendered labels, numbers or symbols.
- [ ] No tool/version/date/prompt/provenance record.
- [ ] No human verification of labels, arrows, data and claims.
- [ ] Graphical abstract or cover without editor permission.
- [ ] Figure implies an experiment that was not performed.
- [ ] AI image policy not checked for the target journal.

## 12. Decision workflow

1. Is the figure a data figure? Use code and real data; no generative image model.
2. Is it a conceptual schematic? Check journal policy; generate layout only; rebuild labels/arrows in vector; label conceptual; disclose.
3. Is it a graphical abstract or cover? Ask the editor; check policy; disclose; verify rights.
4. Did AI assist layout or processing? Record tool/version; disclose if substantive; keep the final editable file.
5. Does the figure pass human QC and the negative checklist? If not, rebuild it.
