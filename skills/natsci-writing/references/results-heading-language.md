# Results subheadings: object, process, finding

Version 1.0; added in NatSci-Writing-skills 1.2.8.1 (2026-11-27).

A Results subheading is a scientific label, not a compressed slogan. It tells a reader who has not read the paper what system was examined and what was measured. The heading must remain understandable when the figure is removed.

## Rules

1. Name the object or system and the measured process or outcome.
2. Use nouns from the paper's terminology ledger: peptide growth, terminal structures, CATH topologies, reaction pools, fold enrichment, dose response, MD stability.
3. Avoid verb-only slogans, metaphors, undefined local shorthand and nominalized abstract nouns.
4. Keep the heading at or below 60 characters and at or below twelve words.
5. Do not open with We, Here, This study or a question; do not use title case.
6. Match the evidence: descriptive headings may state a measured pattern; causal headings require a causal design.
7. Keep grammatical form parallel across Results subsections.
8. Order headings as system, perturbation, structural breadth, reaction conditions, controls, boundary.
9. Do not use a term in a heading before it is defined in the text.
10. If the heading cannot be clear in 60 characters, shorten the claim, not the object.

## Bad and good forms

- Rules bias exploration and folding -> Peptide growth in structure space
- Route horizon reshapes endpoints -> Route scoring changes terminal structures
- A structural atlas of 414 endpoints -> Endpoints occupy 44 CATH topologies
- Open reaction pools sustain folds -> Reaction pools sustain structural lineages
- Controls isolate rule-driven folding -> Fold enrichment survives controls
- Prebiotic chemistry and stability -> Dose response and MD stability

The bad forms fail because the subject is missing, a local metaphor is treated as a term, the verb promises more than the design, or the heading names a method rather than a finding. The good forms name a concrete object and a measured outcome without inventing a second vocabulary.

## Audit

List every Results H3 and answer: what object, what measurement, what condition? If one answer is missing, rewrite. Then check length, parallelism and consistency with the figure legend title. Heading audit is separate from the prose-style gate.
